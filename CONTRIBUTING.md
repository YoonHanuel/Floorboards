# Contributing to Floorboards

Bring one creak, not one thesis.

A good Floorboards specimen is small enough that another person can understand the observation, see the tempting conclusion, identify the hidden assumption, and perform the proposed ferret without first adopting an entire architecture.

## A useful specimen has

- **observed** — claims that can be defended as observations;
- **concluded** — the attractive interpretation someone actually made or might reasonably make;
- **floorboard** — the hidden assumption connecting observation to conclusion;
- **missing_evidence** — what would be needed before the conclusion deserved its confidence;
- **ferret** — a perturbation that should make competing explanations disagree.

The ferret is the important part.

“Gather more data” is usually not a ferret.

“Move only the camera while holding the body stationary” is a ferret.

“Test edge cases” is not a ferret.

“Put two candidates at exactly equal distance and inspect whether the public representation can preserve the tie” is a ferret.

## Please do not

Do not turn one specimen into a universal ontology of epistemology.

Do not repair the underlying product inside this repository. Floorboards records the experimental shape; the actual fix belongs where the actual system lives.

Do not rewrite a historical specimen after later evidence arrives. Add a museum note, resolution, or successor specimen instead.

Do not confuse a funny name with weak rigor. The Human Ferret may be wearing a tiny hat, but the variable isolation still matters.

## Museum admission policy

A solved specimen belongs in `museum/` when at least one of these is true:

- the failure mode is likely to recur elsewhere;
- the experiment taught a reusable distinction;
- the bug report contains language too funny to lose;
- somebody said “there is no way the system is doing *that*” immediately before proving that the system was, in fact, doing that.

## Final check

Before submitting a specimen, ask:

> What result would make me abandon my preferred explanation?

If the answer is “nothing,” you have not brought a floorboard.

You have brought a religion.
